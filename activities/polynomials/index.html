Standard form and the discriminant

A quadratic polynomial has the standard form ax² + bx + c, where a is a non-zero real number and b, c are any real numbers. If a = 0, it is no longer quadratic.

The discriminant is D = b² − 4ac. It tells you how many real zeroes the polynomial has:

  • D > 0 — two distinct real zeroes (the graph crosses the x-axis twice)
  • D = 0 — one repeated real zero (the graph just touches the x-axis)
  • D < 0 — no real zeroes (the graph never meets the x-axis)

Zeroes and coefficients

If α and β are the zeroes of ax² + bx + c, then:

  • Sum of zeroes: α + β = −b⁄a
  • Product of zeroes: α · β = c⁄a

This also works in reverse: given the sum and product of the zeroes, the polynomial is x² − (sum)x + (product), up to a constant multiple.

Worked example

Question: Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 4 and −3.
Step 1: Sum = 4 + (−3) = 1
Step 2: Product = 4 × (−3) = −12
Step 3: Polynomial = x² − (sum)x + (product) = x² − x − 12

Check: x² − x − 12 = (x − 4)(x + 3), which is zero at x = 4 and x = −3 ✓

Shape of the graph

  • If a > 0, the parabola opens upward (a minimum point).
  • If a < 0, the parabola opens downward (a maximum point).
  • The vertex sits on the axis of symmetry, x = −b⁄(2a).

Case Study 1 — Fountain Jet

A jet of water from a garden fountain follows the path h(t) = −t² + 6t, where h is the height in metres and t is the time in seconds after the water leaves the nozzle.

Case Study 2 — Parabolic Arch

An architect is designing a parabolic arch. In a cross-section drawn on a coordinate grid (x and y in metres), the arch meets the ground at x = −4 and x = 4, and the very top of the arch is directly above the midpoint, at the point (0, 8).
